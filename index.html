RICO, defined. 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c) makes it unlawful for any person "employed by or associated with any enterprise engaged in… interstate or foreign commerce, to conduct or participate… in the conduct of such enterprise's affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity." A "pattern" (§ 1961(5)) means at least two predicate acts within ten years. Predicate acts (§ 1961(1)) include wire fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343) and money laundering (18 U.S.C. § 1956). This report asks one question and lets you answer it: does the structure below look like an enterprise conducting its affairs through a repeated pattern of those acts?

§ 1961(4) — The "Enterprise": one identifiable structure, four layers

An enterprise under RICO is "any… group of individuals associated in fact." The on-chain footprint resolves into a single associated-in-fact structure with division of labor. Centralized exchange wallets are labeled terminals (KYC boundary, off-chain), not members of the enterprise. The trail of funds ends at the exchange; who withdrew is known only to the exchange.

§ 1961(1) Predicate Act #1 — Wire fraud via wash trading (18 U.S.C. § 1343)

Wash trading — buying and selling the same asset with no change in beneficial ownership to fabricate the appearance of activity — is explicitly prohibited as market manipulation by the Commodity Exchange Act § 4c(a) (CFTC) and, where securities are involved, by § 9(a)(2) of the Exchange Act (SEC). When fictitious prints are pushed across interstate wires to induce others to buy, each transmission is a separate wire fraud predicate. Two is a pattern. The enterprise generates them by the thousand.
